# Writing style for integPRO content

All site and blog copy must read as if an experienced ISO and DISP consultant wrote it, not an AI assistant.

## Avoid
- Generic, over-polished corporate introductions.
- Repetitive, templated sentence structures.
- Filler transitions: "furthermore", "moreover", "additionally", "therefore", "in today's world", "in conclusion", "it is important to note".
- Empty significance claims: "plays a vital role", "pivotal moment", "key cornerstone", "underscores the importance", "stands as a testament", "reflects broader trends", "evolving landscape", "ever-changing environment", "game-changing", "cutting-edge", "industry-leading".
- Marketing language without evidence, buzzword lists, conclusions that just restate the intro, rhetorical questions used to drive engagement, hype, and manufactured urgency ("must act now", "critical risk").
- Formulaic problem → solution → benefit paragraphs.

## Australian English
- Australian spelling (organisation, realise, licence/license, favour, colour, centre). Use "use", not "utilise" or "utilize", and never "gotten".
- Plain, no-nonsense Australian business English. Use local terms where they fit naturally ("get on the front foot", "cut through"), and don't force slang.
- Reference Australian contexts: Defence Industry Security Program (DISP), ASIC, Australian manufacturers, defence suppliers, SMEs, tech companies.
- Address the reader directly ("You'll need to", "Your organisation can").

## Principles
- Put practical advice first. Be specific: name standards, clauses, risks and outcomes.
- Write as a consultant sharing experience, not as a marketer selling.
- Be honest about complexity and limits. Don't promise certification outcomes.
- Keep paragraphs to 2–4 sentences, and front-load the key point.
- Use headings that match real reader questions. Use bullets only for distinct items. Add an FAQ only where it answers real questions.

## SEO
- Write for search intent first and keywords second, and never stuff keywords.
- Link to internal integPRO pages. Don't link to external sites unless asked.
- Answer the why and the how, not just the what.

## Pre-publish checklist
- Sounds human and conversational, with varied pacing.
- Gives specific, actionable value with real examples.
- Has no AI clichés or marketing fluff.
- Uses Australian English and Australian context.
- Respects the reader and is honest about risks and limitations.
